Parents often ask me what is actually good for their kids online. I understand why. Kids today grow up with phones and tablets, and there is so much out there that it is hard to know where to start. As someone who works in a public library and studies library science, I want to share some of the resources I trust. Many of them are free, and most you can get with just a library card.

## Read the book, then watch the movie

A lot of children's favorites have become movies or TV shows, like *Charlotte's Web*, *Matilda*, *Diary of a Wimpy Kid*, and *Percy Jackson*. This is a fun way to get kids excited about reading. Try reading the book together first, then watch the movie and talk about what changed. Your library usually has both.

## Listen on the go

Audiobooks are great for car rides, bedtime, or kids who are still building their reading skills. Most libraries have a free app where you can borrow audiobooks right on your phone. If you are not sure what to pick, look up the Odyssey Award. It goes to the best audiobooks for children and teens every year.

## Find information you can trust

Google is not always the best place for homework. Libraries pay for research databases made just for kids, and you can use them for free with your library card. There are also great free websites. One of my favorites is the International Children's Digital Library, which has free children's books from around the world in many languages. It is a wonderful resource for families who speak more than one language at home.

## Make something new

Screens do not have to be only for watching. Kids can write, draw, make comics, record music, or make short videos. Scratch is a free tool where kids can learn to code by building their own games and stories. One tip: free online tools sometimes disappear, so remind kids to save their work somewhere else too.

## Apps worth trying

Every year, librarians from the American Library Association choose the best digital media for kids 14 and under. Here are a few from the list:

- **Duolingo** turns language learning into a game with short lessons and rewards.
- **Classics for Kids** introduces children to classical music, composers, and instruments through short shows and games.
- **Inside U** uses characters from Pixar's *Inside Out* to help kids understand their feelings.
- **Lyla in the Loop** is a PBS KIDS show and app that teaches young kids problem-solving in a fun way.

## How to choose

Before you hand your child a new app or website, ask yourself a few questions. Is it right for my child's age and reading level? Does it use pictures, sound, and video, not just text? Can my child use it on their own, or will they need help? Is it free, or will it ask for money later? The best way to find out is to try it together first.

## Ask your librarian

This is my favorite tip. Librarians love helping people find good resources, and many libraries make online guides on popular topics. If you are looking for something specific, just ask. That is what we are here for.
