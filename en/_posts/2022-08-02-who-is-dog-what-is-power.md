**The “Power of the Dog” movie review**

After Jane Campion’s first film, “the piano”, which won the Palme d’Or, her fans were eagerly awaiting another masterpiece. She didn’t let her fans down with her film, “The power of the dog.”

This noir western is based on a novel by the American Thomas Savage that was published in 1967. Taking place in 1925 Montana, the movie centers around the family cattle ranch run by Phil (Benedict Cumberbatch) and George Burbank. George leads the farm as a manager while Phil handles the farm’s day-to-day operations. The plot centers on what happens when George marries Rose (Kirsten Dunst), a widowed mother with a teenage son named Peter (Kodi Smit-McPhee). Phil hates Rose, considering her an opportunist. Rose and Phil begin a secret feud and use every opportunity to show how much they hate one another. As a result, Phil tries to get Rose’s son to bother her, which would have negative consequences for him.

The movie generally shows contradictions. In the movie, themes such as inside and outside, gentleness and violence, tradition and modernity are well explored. Through various situations, we get a glimpse into the characters’ subconscious contrasts. For example, Peter makes paper flowers and has a girlish spirit at the beginning of the movie. However, when the film continues, he mutilates a rabbit or suffocates another rabbit with his hand. Phil dresses in dirty clothes and behaves violently. He castrates the horses with his hands and then shows a gentle side as if he is in love with an old master or playing music. Rose appears to be kind and trustworthy, which calms her husband’s life. However, she becomes a highly unreliable, alcoholic woman who does strange things. Also seen in the film is the contrast of wildlife with a stylish house with books, chess, and musical instruments.

Throughout “Power of the Dog”, we see a triangle consisting of Phil, Peter, and Branco. All three of these people (from three different generations) have something in common, and that is that they are the only ones who can see a “dog” on the mountain! The question in the audience’s mind is why these three people see the dog and the others cannot. Why are they special? And what does this dog symbolize?

A big part of this movie is Cumberbatch’s direction and the direction of Campion, along with his precise and attractive performance. Considering that Campion is an excellent director of acting, it is no surprise that the actors in this movie have all performed their best performances. Cumberbatch’s character and performance are particularly remarkable. In this movie, Cumberbatch can be portrayed as a villain, but the point is, as bad as he is, you can’t dislike him. Cumberbatch gives this character an unbearably charismatic quality.

The character of Rose is one of the movie’s greatest weaknesses. While Christine Dunst was trying hard to portray an unstable alcoholic woman, her actions and reactions were out of character and uncharacteristic. As viewers, we can’t get familiar with her personality as well as her behavior.

While there are no direct scenes that show homosexuality, there are signs throughout the movie. Peter finds a wooden box containing magazines featuring naked men in the scene that belonged to Branco Henry or it was a sweet memory for Phil to hug Branco on a cold night. Does Phil have a secret? Were he and Branco Henry once in love? Campion subtly sets these questions at the story's center, and the movie is open to interpretations of such questions.

The movie’s ending is shocking and unexpected. The audience is left with a lot of questions that you might need to watch the movie again to find the answers to! For recommendation, if campion had worked better on Rose’s personality, we would have seen a better conflict between her and Phil.

Grade: 92/100
