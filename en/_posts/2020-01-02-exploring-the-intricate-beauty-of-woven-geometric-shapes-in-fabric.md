Nobody knows where the warp and weft first appeared and the history of weaving.; But for years, this has artistically perpetuated many symbols. However, according to history, “ weaving” is very old in Mazandaran and dates back to five thousand years BC. There are some signs in the “Kamar-Band” cave, which is located in Behshahr, that indicate spinning wool into yarn.

One of the most important handicrafts of Mazandaran province, which consists of geometric shapes is called “Metkazin Baafi” (a kind of rug-weaving). This type of rug, which is one of the original and beautiful handicrafts of Mazandaran, was created in ancient times by picking wool and spinning yarn, and the weavers put a mental design on the rug without having a plan.

The ancients believed that the reason for naming this rug ‘’Metkazin’’ was its use for the saddle of horses, which was called by this name in the local dialect. To weave this rug, a hand-made machine was used that could pass the warps through the weft on its surface to make geometric patterns. It mainly has two sizes and is woven in dimensions of 100*60 or 150*90 cm, and for larger sizes, usually, two or three rugs are connected. Nowadays, cotton yarn and wool are used for weaving this kind of rug.

If you meet Metkazin weavers and ask them about the famous patterns they make on these beautiful handicrafts, they will reply that Metkazin designs have long been inspired by the nature from ancient times. For more elaboration, they would mention some famous designs. These designs have been created completely empirically and have been transferred to weavers since ancient times. According to Metkazin weavers, the designs of this rug have four main categories. The first group is basic geometric shapes including square, triangle, and diamond designs. The most famous design of this group is the “Naqsh-e Chahar Fasl”. The second group is plant-inspired geometric shapes (inspired by trees). The next group is natural geometric shapes, which is a simplified pattern of sun, mountains, etc. The fourth group is inspired by the most famous animal forms, such as beehives and animal paws. Also, designs such as dark sun, beehive shape, bloom, etc. that each of them falls into one of the above categories, are more common than other designs.

In addition to the beautiful and unique designs, the color used in this kind of rug is also very important. For weaving Metkazin, they usually use red, green, pale goldenrod, dark blue, black, yellow, and indigo; and one of the most popular colors for weaving is crimson. In the past, this type of rug was used as the cover of “Korsi” (Iranian foot stove), doormat or groundsheet, and even to wrap the blankets and pillows, which today has a decorative aspect. These original and beautiful handicrafts received the seal of excellence and authenticity from UNESCO in 1989 and were registered nationally.
