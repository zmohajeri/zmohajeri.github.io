If we were to summarize the philosophy of Stoicism in one phrase, it would mean finding peace in life despite all difficulties. Stoics believe that we should live with reason and logic, not with [[1]](#_ftn1)emotions. We must learn how to control our thoughts and emotions and behave in a way that aligns with our values. Stoicism emphasizes four main virtues: wisdom, courage, justice, and temperance.

This philosophy teaches us how to face the hardships and challenges of life while maintaining our tranquility. The most important practice in Stoicism is distinguishing between what we can change and what we cannot. There are two types of things in life: those within our control and those outside our control. Stoicism teaches us to accept what we cannot control and, instead of worrying, focus on what we can control. As Epictetus says, "The most important choice we have in life is between two things: to be concerned with things outside ourselves or with things inside ourselves. Happiness can never coexist with the desire for things we do not have."

This means that while most people seek satisfaction by changing the world around them, Stoics achieve it by changing their desires and inner selves.

**My Personal Philosophy**

My personal philosophy in life is based on two important psychological concepts: resilience and choice theory. The first, resilience, is the ability to cope with difficult situations and respond flexibly to life's pressures. It empowers individuals to face problems healthily, overcome hardships, and adapt to unexpected situations. Choice theory, on the other hand, posits that the more our internal control is strengthened and stabilized, the fewer problems and challenges we will encounter in life. In other words, while we cannot have complete control over external events, we can control our reactions and attitudes towards them. Both of these concepts are similar to the philosophy of Stoicism. They all believe that by focusing more on ourselves and gaining greater self-mastery, we can overcome the problems, sufferings, and difficulties in our surroundings.

Some Stoic meditation practices include three parts: first, focusing on what we have and being grateful for it; second, recognizing what we can control and focusing on our reactions to external events; and third, remembering death as a method to create priorities, meaning, and urgency in life. I have incorporated these practices, especially gratitude and focusing on what I can control, into my life and strive to live by them. We must all accept responsibility for our actions and embrace our current situation with all its good and bad aspects.

In my religious practices, there is a prayer that says, "Lord, grant me the serenity to accept the things I cannot change, the courage to change the things I can, and the wisdom to know the difference." I believe this belief originates from the Stoic philosophical tenets. Without this belief, a person would constantly struggle to attain things they might never achieve, experiencing exhaustion and suffering that could rob them of their peace. And if they fail to achieve them, they might feel frustrated and defeated. In general, Stoicism can provide valuable tools for managing stress, increasing life satisfaction, and attaining peace and wisdom in the face of life's daily challenges.
